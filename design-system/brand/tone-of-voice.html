Verifi Design System/Brand/Tone of voice

Tone of voice.

Clear and simple, confident and intelligent, grounded in operations. Verifi speaks with the authority of a company that knows concrete in the real world, and the clarity of a technology company that turns complexity into decisions.

What changed in this version (October 2026). New page, from the Tone of Voice chapter (October 2026). It covers marketing and external copy: web, press, LinkedIn, sales decks, proposals, case studies and customer emails. It is also the brief to give Claude before drafting anything for Verifi; see Drafting with Claude. Product copy inside the Hub (labels, helper text, empty states) follows the component pages, not this one.
Voice stays the same everywhere; tone flexes by channel and reader. We make sophisticated technology simple to understand and act on, without talking down to people who know concrete. The ideal sentence sounds like it came from someone who knows what it takes to batch and deliver concrete, and what technology can do to help. Every piece should leave two impressions: we know concrete, and we know where concrete is going.

The five tests

Run every claim through these before it is published. A claim that fails one is rewritten, not softened.

Can we picture it?a rejected loadoperational transformation
Can we prove it?more than 6,000 trucksunmatched performance
Does the customer care?less waiting time at the jobsitecloud-native architecture
Could any technology company say it?know the slump before the truck reaches the pourunlock actionable insights
Does it move from information to action?see which loads need attention nowaccess real-time dashboards

Six attributes

Each one is a pair: the thing we are, and the thing we slide into when we try too hard.

  • Clearnot complicated
  • Confidentnot grandiose
  • Intelligentnot clever
  • Operationalnot abstract
  • Progressivenot futuristic
  • Humannot corporate

Quote these verbatim. They are not rewritten for a channel, a region or a deck.

Approved descriptions

Use as written.

Verifi gives concrete producers real-time visibility and control over every load, from plant to pour.

The press boilerplate and its short version live with the Tone of Voice chapter; they are not reproduced here so there is one copy to keep current.

Readers

Pick a persona before writing a word: Management, Operations, Dispatch, Quality control, Batch operator, Driver, and on the other side of the pour, contractors and owners. Persona names never appear in customer copy; they are how we choose what to say, not what we call people.

  • Do not take sides. Producer and contractor end up looking at the same record. Copy that cheers for one of them loses the other.
  • Promise help, not perfection. Verifi catches, corrects and shows. It does not abolish.
  • Write for a global reader. No idioms, no sports, no local shorthand.

Value and story

Four levels of impact, in order

  1. End-to-end visibility — what is happening to every load, from plant to pour.
  2. Effective operations — fewer waits, fewer returns, fewer calls.
  3. Materials optimization — less cement, water and admixture to hit the same spec.
  4. Commercial uplift — what the first three are worth.

Land on at least one. Order inside a piece is mechanism, then money, then carbon. Prove it or describe it; never estimate or invent economics.

Story spine

Reality → Consequence → What Verifi does → What changes → Proof → Confidence

Open in the customer’s world, not ours. Write about decisions, not dashboards.

Words

Words we own: confidence, clarity, trust, visibility, control, consistency, every load, plant to pour, proof. Prefer verbs. No absolutes: eliminate, guarantee, zero.

The numbers that are approved for use. Anything not on this list needs a named source or a customer who has signed off.

Checklist

Every answer is yes before copy is approved. Humans approve quotes, customer stories and every number, and read the draft aloud before it ships.

  • Does the reader get the point on first reading?
  • Does it open in the customer’s world, not ours?
  • Have we said what Verifi actually does, in plain terms?
  • Does the technology connect to a decision or a result?
  • Does it land on at least one of the four levels of impact?
  • Is every claim backed by an approved number, a named source or a customer?
  • Have we removed absolutes, stacked adjectives and “innovation” language?
  • Could a vague noun become a strong verb?
  • Does it respect the expertise of concrete professionals?
  • One spelling system, one unit system, sentence-case headings, correct product names?
  • Can any sentence be shorter?
  • Does it leave the reader more confident?

Drafting with Claude

Give it this page, one or two approved examples of the asset, and the facts: customer, numbers, quote approvals, date. It uses only the facts supplied. It never invents statistics, customer names, quotes or results; anything missing comes back as [CONFIRM: what is needed], followed by a list of any rules it was unsure how to apply.

The brief

  • Asset and channelWeb page, press release, LinkedIn post, deck slide, proposal section, case study, email
  • ReaderOne persona, named here and nowhere in the copy
  • The one pointWhat the reader should take away
  • Value levelWhich of the four to land on
  • ProofWhich approved numbers, sources or customers
  • Who is speakingVerifi, a named person, a customer
  • Region and unitsUS, UK, global
  • Length and CTAWord count or format, and what the reader does next

Review prompts that work

  • Check this draft line by line against the tone of voice guide and name the rule each line breaks.
  • Run the five tests on every claim.
  • Rewrite for a different persona without changing a fact.
  • Cut by a third and keep every number.
  • Give five sentence-case headline options, one idea each.
