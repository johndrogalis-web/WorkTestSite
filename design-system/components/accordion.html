Accordion

A section that opens and closes under a heading. The heading is the button; the content is whatever you put in it.

LearnWhen to reach for it, and a live one to try.

When to use

Use it for

  • A long page where each person needs only one or two sections, such as truck settings.
  • Optional detail most people skip, such as history or advanced settings.
  • A list of similar sections that people scan by title.

Use something else when

  • People need to compare sections side by side. Use Tabs, or show them all.
  • The content is two or three short paragraphs. Show it.
  • It is the main content of the page. Show it.
  • It is a task that has to be finished. Use a Modal or Drawer.
  • It is one summary with one action. Use a Card.

Try it

Click a header, or tab to it and press Enter or Space. The switches turn parts on and off.

Light

Number of drum magnets: 3. Drum size: 3. Front discharge on. Water meter on, admix meter on, watchdog off.

Dispatch by SMS for slump out of range. Plant by email for every returned load.

Twelve changes, the most recent yesterday at 14:20 by M. Alvarez.

UnderstandWhat it does by itself, and the choices you make.

Behaviour

What the accordion does by itself. Nothing here needs a decision.

  • The whole header opens and closes it. The chevron only shows which way it will go.
  • Sections are independent. Opening one leaves the others alone.
  • Only the header reacts to hover, focus and press. The content never changes colour, so it is always clear what Enter will act on.
  • The content keeps its own styles. The accordion adds no padding or dividers inside the slot.
  • Disabled cannot be focused or opened. It stays in place so the reason next to it makes sense.
  • Hover and focus change in 150ms. Opening and closing take 300ms. Both ease out. Under reduced motion both are instant.

Guidelines

The choices you make, and why.

  • Start closed. If the page is about one section, open that one and only that one.
  • Write titles that say what is inside. Nobody should have to open a section to find out.
  • Add the count when the number is the reason to open. Changes, items or alerts, as a number. Never a word that repeats the title.
  • Use Disabled only when the section will become available. Say why next to it. If it will never be available to this person, leave it out.
  • Leave the icon off unless it helps people find a section. Turn it on for three or more different sections that each have their own icon. All or none in a group, never with the chevron at the start, and never to show status.
  • Keep the chevron at the end. Use the start for a list read top to bottom. One position per screen.
  • Use one-at-a-time only when sections replace each other. Steps are the usual case. Everywhere else, let people open as many as they need.

Do not

  • Hide the main content of the page in a closed accordion.
  • Put an accordion inside an accordion.
  • Use it for two or three short paragraphs.
  • Split a form that has to be submitted across closed sections.
Building itAnatomy, options, states, numbers and code, for designers and developers.

Anatomy

  • ContainerThe frame around the header and the content.
  • HeaderThe button. It takes hover, focus and press.
  • TitleAlways shown. Says what is inside.
  • SubheadingOptional. One line of detail under the title.
  • IconOptional. Before the title.
  • CountOptional. A Badge in the Alternate style.
  • ChevronAlways shown. Points down when closed, up when open.
  • ContentA slot. Anything goes in, and the accordion styles none of it.

Accessibility

  • The header is a <button> inside a heading, with aria-expanded and aria-controls.
  • The content is role="region", labelled by the header.
  • Enter and Space open and close. Tab moves between headers.
  • The focus ring bounds the header only (WCAG 2.4.13).
  • The count is read as part of the button name: “Truck settings, 3 changes, collapsed”. Write it so it reads well.
  • Disabled is exempt from the contrast minimum (WCAG 1.4.3), which is why the reason has to be written next to it.

Every text pair passes AA; the lowest is 12.02:1. The focus ring reads 13.46:1 light and 10.45:1 dark against the hover fill.

In the Angular code the Accordion is one ready-made piece. Every colour, size and state on this page is already inside it, so the person building a screen chooses from the settings below and never restyles anything.

  • What you type<vf-accordion>
  • What you can setopen (boolean), title, subtitle, count (number), chevron (one of: end, start), icon (optional icon name)
  • What it tells youopenChange (boolean)
  • What it handles for youThe header is a <button aria-expanded aria-controls> inside a heading; the panel is role="region" aria-labelledby and is hidden when closed. Content is projected with <ng-content>.
