Accordion

A section that opens and closes under a heading. The heading is the button; the content is whatever you put in it. One size, two chevron positions, and a slot for the content.

SolidHeader + slotLight and darkFigma 63692:9304 ↗
Two parts, and only the first one reacts. The header is a button: it takes hover, focus and press, and it carries the title, an optional subheading, an optional icon, an optional count and the chevron. The content underneath is a slot — drop in whatever the section holds — and it never changes colour, open or closed. That split is deliberate and is explained under Accessibility. The settings form and “Data Accordion” frames in Figma show the slot in use; they are examples, not part of the component.

Anatomy

  • ContainerThe outer frame: 16px radius, 1px border, 3px inside padding. It holds the header and the content.
  • HeaderThe button. 16px radius, 6px top and bottom padding, 8px left and right, 8px gap between its parts. 66px tall with a subheading, 48px without.
  • TitleABC Repro Medium 24px
  • SubheadingOptional. ABC Repro Regular 16px, under the title
  • IconOptional, 24px, before the title
  • CountOptional. A Badge in the Alternate style, after the title
  • Chevron24px, at the end of the header by default, or at the start. Points down when closed, up when open.
  • ContentA slot with 10px padding. Anything goes in; nothing about it is styled by the accordion.

Behaviour

  • The whole header opens and closes the section. Not just the chevron. The chevron only shows which way it will go.
  • Sections are independent by default. Opening one does not close another. Use one-at-a-time only when the sections are alternatives, such as steps that replace each other.
  • Start closed unless the page is about that section, in which case open the first one and only the first one.
  • Hover, focus and press live on the header only. The content below stays plain whether it is open or closed. The reasons are under Accessibility.
  • The count says how much is inside — changes, items, alerts. It is a Badge and follows the badge rules: a number, never a word that repeats the title.
  • The content is a slot. The accordion styles nothing inside it. If the content needs its own padding or dividers, they belong to the content.
  • Opening and closing take 0.14s, the same as every colour change. Under reduced motion the panel simply appears.

Guidelines

Do

  • Use it to shorten a long page where only one or two sections matter to any one person.
  • Write titles that say what is inside, so nobody has to open a section to find out.
  • Put the count on when the number is the reason someone would open it.
  • Keep one chevron position per screen.

Do not

  • Do not hide the only important content on a page inside a closed accordion.
  • Do not nest an accordion inside an accordion.
  • Do not use it for two or three short paragraphs. Show them.
  • Do not put a form that must be submitted across several closed sections. Nobody will find the empty required field.
  • Do not make the content react to hover or focus. Only the header does.

Accessibility

Every text pair passes with room to spare. The one thing that needed a change was the focus ring: the Figma token is a mid neutral that measures 1.59:1 against the header it sits on, so the ring is specified here in the page ink instead, which measures 13:1.

Why hover and focus stay on the header. Keyboard users need the highlight to show exactly what Enter will do. Highlight the whole section — header plus open content — and it is no longer clear whether Enter closes the section or activates something inside it. Screen readers announce whatever the highlight bounds: scoped to the header it is a clear button, scoped to the section it is a vague group. Touch has no hover at all, so scoping to the header keeps the behaviour the same on every input. This is the standard, not a preference: WCAG 2.4.13 asks the focus indicator to bound the actual control, and Material, Carbon and Lightning all do it this way.
  • The header is a <button> inside a heading. Buttons get keyboard and screen-reader behaviour for free; a div with a click handler gets none of it.
  • aria-expanded on the button says open or closed. aria-controls points at the content.
  • The content is a role="region" labelled by the header, so a screen reader user can jump to it.
  • Enter and Space toggle. Arrow keys are not required; Tab moves between headers.
  • The count badge is read as part of the button’s name: “Truck settings, 3 changes”. Write it so that reads well.
  • The border and the hover fill are low-contrast on purpose; they are decoration. The ring and the text carry the state.

Known gaps

Solid, as a starting point. The header and the slot are specified, every state has a value, every colour pair passes, and the rules for when to use one are written down. Nothing here blocks a build.

Three tidy-ups for the Figma file, none of which change what is specified here: point accordion/stroke-focus at neutral/950 light and neutral/150 dark (the current values measure 1.59:1 and 2.64:1; the page carries the passing pair); retire the older 36-variant Accordion set so nobody builds against it; and rename the new set’s Property 1 to Open.
