Verifi Design System/Components/Avatar

Avatar

A person, drawn as a circle or a square, at four sizes. A photograph when there is one, an icon when there is not.

Solid2 shapes4 sizesLight and darkFigma 60899:8165 ↗
Simple on purpose. An avatar shows a person. It has two shapes, four sizes, and one fallback: the icon. There are no initials, no stacks and no status dots, and that is a decision, not a gap. Anything an avatar cannot say on its own, the name beside it says.

Anatomy

  • Circle radius9999
  • Square radius8px
  • Large56 × 56
  • Medium32 × 32
  • Small24 × 24
  • XSmall16 × 16
  • ContentAn image, or the user icon from the icon atom. The icon is 24 at Large, 20 at Medium, 16 at Small and 12 at XSmall, drawn at a 1.5px stroke.
  • XSmall contentIcon only. An image is not permitted at 16px.
  • Ring1px inside the edge, in the light neutral (#F0EEEA) in both themes. It separates a photograph from a dark surface and is invisible on a light one, which is the intent: the ring is for the photo, not for the page.

What each size may contain

XSmall 16No — a face is unreadable at this sizeYes
XSmall is the icon form only. The Figma set has no image variant at XSmall, so there is nothing to misuse. Every XSmall example on this page is an icon for that reason.

States

No interactive states are defined. An avatar inside a clickable row or menu inherits that control’s states; on its own it is a picture.

Behaviour

  • Pick one shape for a product and keep it. Circles and squares mixed on one screen read as two systems.
  • The icon is an instance of the icon atom, not an embedded SVG, so a different icon can be swapped in without touching the component. That is also how the no-photo case is handled: there is no initials variant, and there is not meant to be one. Where two people need telling apart, the name does it.
  • Do not put a photograph in the XSmall avatar. At 16px a face is a smudge. Figma no longer has an image variant at XSmall; the icon is the only form.
  • The image fills the frame, cropped square from the centre. There is no focal-point control, so ask for square source images where you can.
  • If the image is missing or fails to load, the icon form shows. Never a broken image and never an empty circle.
  • A status (online, away, unread) does not sit on the avatar. Put a badge beside the name instead; a dot on a 16px avatar has nowhere to go.
  • Several people are listed, one avatar per row, not stacked. Stacks need an overlap rule, an order and an overflow count, and none of those exist here.

Guidelines

Do

  • Choose one shape per product.
  • Use the icon form when there is no photograph. It is the intended fallback, not a placeholder.
  • Match the size to the density of what surrounds it.

Do not

  • Do not mix circle and square avatars in one interface.
  • Do not use a photograph at XSmall. Use the icon form.
  • Do not stretch an image to fit. It crops, by design.
  • Do not rely on the avatar alone to identify someone. Put the name near it.

  • Background, dark#F0EEEA

Accessibility

Both themes clear the 4.5:1 requirement with a wide margin. The ring is not measured: it is not a control boundary and carries no meaning, so the 3:1 rule does not apply to it. It exists to keep a photograph from merging with a dark surface, and against a dark photo it is 14:1 or better.

  • An avatar that carries meaning needs alt text with the person’s name. A decorative one beside a visible name takes alt="".
  • The icon form is decorative when a name sits next to it. Do not announce “user icon” twice.
  • If the avatar is the only way to tell two people apart, that is a failure regardless of contrast. Show the name.
  • An avatar inside a link or button must not swallow that control’s accessible name.

Known gaps

Solid, as a starting point. Every question a builder has is answered: what goes in each size, how an image crops, what shows when there is no image, where a status goes, how a list of people is shown, and how the avatar keeps its edge on a photograph. The ring, the icon sizes and the icon-only XSmall are all in Figma now. Verifi is still discovering its full brand, so this can change — together, and always passing AA.

Figma housekeeping

Things the design file still has to catch up on. None of them change what is specified here.

  • The variant properties read Med and squircle. Rename to Medium and Square so the file, this page and the code agree.
  • The user glyph comes from the Feather set. The icon rule says Unicons for new work and Feather frozen; either swap the glyph to its Unicons equivalent or record Avatar as an agreed exception. A one-instance change either way.

Decided against

  • Initials. The icon covers the no-photo case; the name covers telling people apart.
  • Stacked groups. Deprioritised. List people instead.
  • A status dot on the avatar. The badge goes beside the name.
