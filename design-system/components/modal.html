Modal / Drawer

One overlay component with two variants and three behaviours. A centred dialog, a drawer that blocks the page, and a drawer that does not — and a decision tree that tells you which one you need.

Has gaps2 variants3 behavioursLight onlyFigma 64988:61036 ↗A11y doc 64984:60852 ↗
What changed in this version (October 2026). The page was Modal only, one size, a confirm box. It is now Modal / Drawer: Figma restructured it as one component with Default (624) and Mobile (360) variants, a header-content-footer interior, and footer buttons from the Button set. Two new written documents in the file — a Component Specification and an Accessibility Requirements sheet — add the decision tree for dialog vs modal drawer vs non-modal drawer, the ARIA for each, focus rules, motion timings and a WCAG checklist. All of that is on this page now. Dark mode is still unpublished in Figma.
What it is. The layer that sits on top of the page. Sometimes it demands an answer before the page can continue; sometimes it just keeps something useful within reach. The visual shell is the same either way, which is why Figma makes it one component. The behaviour is not the same, which is why the tree matters.

Anatomy

  • Panelmodal/background #FFFFFF, modal/radius 16px, a 1px modal/border #DAD8D7 and a 0 4px 24px shadow in rgba(74,79,85,.12). Drawer squares the corners on the viewport edge it sits on.
  • HeaderTitle in modal/header/text #171614, 20px side padding and 16px top and bottom, a close button on the right, a 1px rule beneath. Width is fill.
  • ContentA slot. modal/default/padding 24px (16px on Mobile). Body/sm 14px Regular. Content-driven height with a max; a scrollbar appears when it overflows.
  • Footermodal/footer/padding 20px, buttons 8px apart, a 1px rule above. Desktop: Secondary left, Link + Primary right. Mobile: stacked full-width, Primary first.
  • ScrimModal only. Dims the page at 50%. A non-modal drawer has none.
  • ButtonsMedium (32px) Buttons: Primary, Secondary and Link.

Options

Modal / Drawer2 variants · 3 behaviours
One component, two variants, three behaviours. Figma builds Default and Mobile as variants of a single component and positions them in code. Whether the thing is a centred dialog, a modal drawer or a non-modal drawer is a behaviour decision, not a visual one — the interior is identical. That is the core principle on the Figma page and this page follows it.

Which one

The Figma file settles this with a decision tree. Two questions, four answers.

Does the user have to finish an action before continuing?ThenUseExamples
YesThe content is complex, long or multi-stepModal drawer — trap focus, scrim, page inert, role="dialog" aria-modal="true", slides from the edgeSettings panel, multi-step form, complex filters, batch views
The content is shortModal dialog — trap focus, scrim, page inert, role="dialog" aria-modal="true", centredConfirmations, alerts, simple forms, warnings
NoThe page behind is still relevantNon-modal drawer — no scrim, no trap, no inert, role="complementary" or <aside>Help panel, contextual info, navigation menu, filters you adjust while watching results
The page behind is not relevantReconsider. It may need a modal after all, a different component, or its own pageContent that replaces the page; full-page takeovers
The rule most teams break. If the page stays visible and usable, the drawer must not trap focus, must not dim the page, and must not block interaction. Applying modal behaviour to every drawer is the single most common accessibility failure in this pattern.

For developers. Nothing in this section changes how the Modal looks or behaves. If you design in Figma or write the rules, you can skip it.

One component, three modes. The mode decides the role, the scrim, the trap and inert; nothing else differs.

States

A modal has two states, closed and open, and most of what matters happens in the transition. Open: focus lands on the title. Content taller than the max height scrolls inside the body while the header and footer stay put. Closed: it leaves the DOM, or if it must stay, it is display:none so nothing inside it can take focus.

StateWhat changes
OpeningDialog scales from 95% and fades in over 250ms ease-out; drawer slides from its edge over 300ms ease-out; scrim fades over 200ms. Focus moves to the title.
Open, overflowingBody scrolls. Header and footer are fixed. The scrollbar is the only visual cue that there is more.
ClosingDialog scales to 95% and fades over 200ms ease-in; drawer slides out over 250ms ease-in. Focus returns to the trigger. inert and the scroll lock are removed.
Reduced motionNo scale, no slide. Appear and disappear.

Behaviour

These are rules, not suggestions. Everything under Which one, and everything here, is specified in the Figma file’s Component Specification and Accessibility Requirements. Build to this.
  • Decide the behaviour with the tree, not with the layout. A drawer is not automatically non-modal and a centred box is not automatically a dialog. Ask whether the user must finish before continuing.
  • Modal means all three: trap, scrim, inert. Trap focus inside, dim the page at 50%, and apply inert to everything behind. Lock page scroll. Any one without the others is a half-modal, and half-modals are where keyboard users get lost.
  • Non-modal means none of the three. No scrim, no trap, no inert, page scroll allowed. Focus flows from the last control in the drawer back into the page.
  • Focus goes to the title on open and back to the trigger on close. The title carries tabindex="-1" so it can take focus without joining the tab order.
  • Escape closes all three. No exceptions. Clicking the scrim closes a dialog; whether it closes a modal drawer is a per-use call, and it never applies to non-modal because there is no scrim.
  • Destructive confirmations keep Cancel on the left and the destructive action on the right, in a Destructive button, never Primary. The safe choice is the one closest to where the pointer already is.
  • Width follows the content. 320 to 960px, chosen per use. A confirmation is narrow; a form is as wide as its longest row and no wider.
  • One at a time. A modal must not open another modal. If a flow needs more than one step, it is a multi-step modal drawer, not a stack.
  • Below 600px, switch to Mobile. 360px, stacked full-width buttons with Primary on top for thumb reach, presented as a bottom sheet.

Guidelines

Do

  • Use a modal dialog for a decision that must be made now: delete, discard, leave without saving.
  • Use a modal drawer for a multi-step task that needs the whole screen’s attention.
  • Use a non-modal drawer for help, filters and details the user reads alongside the page.
  • Put the title in the header and the actions in the footer. Nothing else goes in either.
  • Give every drawer a visible close button, even the non-modal ones.

Do not

  • Do not trap focus or dim the page behind a non-modal drawer.
  • Do not use role="dialog" on something the user can tab out of.
  • Do not open a modal from a modal.
  • Do not use a modal for a message that needs no decision. That is a Notification.
  • Do not leave a closed modal in the tab order. Remove it or hide it.
  • Do not invent a width tier. Size it to the content inside the 320–960 range.

Accessibility

Text on the panel is #171614 on #FFFFFF, 18.08:1. The panel border is #DAD8D7 on white, 1.42:1 — well under the 3:1 that 1.4.11 wants for a component boundary, but the boundary here is carried by the shadow and the scrim, and the border is decoration. The buttons inherit the Button results.

Common failures

  • Focus does not move into the overlay on open, or does not return on close. Both are 2.4.3.
  • No visible focus ring on the close button. 2.4.7.
  • Escape does nothing. Add the keydown listener.
  • A closed overlay still in the tab order. Hide it properly.
  • Non-modal specific: aria-modal="true", role="dialog", a scrim, inert, or a focus trap on a drawer the page is still usable behind. Any one of these turns a non-modal drawer into a broken modal.

Known gaps

  • No dark-mode tokens. The Modal / Drawer page publishes one light set. The dark panes on this page use the site’s surface tokens as a stand-in and are not a Figma value. Needs a dark frame in the file.
  • Content padding: 24 or 20. The variable modal/default/padding is 24; the Component Specification text says 20. This page follows the variable. One of them should change.
  • The two written documents are marked Pre-development research. Their content is specific, consistent and matches established practice, so this page treats it as the spec. Worth lifting the label once it has been built once.
  • Panel border is 1.3:1. #DAD8D7 on white. Fine as decoration under a shadow; not fine if the shadow is ever removed. Consider #C4C1BD or darker if the border has to carry the edge on its own.
  • Scrim colour is not a token. The spec says 50% but not of what. This page uses black. Publish it.
  • Non-modal drawer has no Figma variant. It is the same frame with different behaviour, which is correct, but a designer looking for it in the file will not find it by name.
