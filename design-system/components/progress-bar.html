Verifi Design System/Components/Progress bar

Progress bar

An 8px bar that fills left to right. Eight variants, every one of them named by the value it shows.

Solid8 variantsLight and darkFigma 51692:17330 ↗
The contrast failure is fixed, and the fix is a 1px ring rather than a darker track. A track dark enough to clear the page on its own lands at #7F796C, and at that value no recognisable status colour can clear 3:1 against it — a fill would have to be near-black or pastel. So the track stays light and a 1px ring at #7F796C carries the bar’s extent instead. That buys both boundaries and leaves the track free for status.
This is an atom, and it stays one. One component, eight variants. The bar carries no text of its own — a label and a number belong to whatever places the bar, and the spec for them is under Anatomy so that six screens do not invent six formats. Status, size and both motion durations are tokens on the same component, not separate components.

Anatomy

  • TrackFull width of its container, 8px tall, 4px radius
  • Ring1px inside the track, #7F796C in both modes. This is what defines the bar’s full extent against the page.
  • FillSame height and radius, width set by the value
  • Height tokenprogress bar/height = 8, which is spacing/micro/200
  • Radius tokenprogress bar/radius = 4, which is radius/sm
  • Label and valueNot part of the component. Where the caller adds them: Body/sm label on the left, Body/xs value on the right, 6px clear of the track, the two ends aligned to the bar. Round to whole percent.

States

A progress bar has no interaction states — no hover, no focus, no disabled. It has value states, and there are three.

StateWhat it showsWhat it announces
DeterminateA fill from 0 to 100, and a number.aria-valuenow with min and max.
IndeterminateA 30% sliver travelling left to right, and no number.aria-valuenow is removed, and aria-busy="true" is set. An indeterminate bar reporting 0 is a bar claiming no progress, which is a different and wrong statement.
SettledThe fill stops and stays where it stopped, at 100 on success or wherever it failed.The final aria-valuenow, plus the outcome in the label.
A failed bar does not reset and does not keep crawling. It stops at the last real number and says what went wrong. Snapping back to 0 erases the only evidence the user has of how far the job got, and a bar that keeps creeping after the work has died is the single most distrusted pattern in any progress UI.

Behaviour

Movement

  • Width only, 240ms, cubic-bezier(.22, 1, .36, 1). The system ease. Nothing else about the bar moves — no pulse, no shimmer, no stripe.
  • Never animate backwards. If a new estimate is lower than the current fill, hold where you are until the real number catches up. A bar that goes back reads as failure even when it is just a better estimate.
  • Indeterminate travels on a 1.6s loop — a 30% sliver crossing the track. Slower than a spinner on purpose, because it is furniture, not a focal point.
  • Under prefers-reduced-motion, neither moves. The determinate bar jumps to its value; the indeterminate one becomes a static filled track at 55% opacity. Something that slides across the screen forever is exactly what that setting is for.

Status is not a colour

The bar has one fill. Trinity carries success, warning and error fills as tokens, and they are listed under Specs, but swapping the fill is the last thing that happens when a job succeeds or fails, not the first.

  • The outcome is carried by the words beside the bar. A bar that turns red and says nothing tells a colourblind user that something happened, not what. WCAG 1.4.1 Use of Colour, A.
  • If you only change one of the two, change the words. A failure stated in text and left in the default fill is correct. A failure shown only as a red bar is not.
  • There is no warning fill in normal use. “Slower than usual” is a sentence, not a colour. Amber on a bar that is still working reads as a fault.
  • Success rarely needs a colour at all. The bar reaching 100 with a label that says so is already unambiguous.
The bar has no text, and gains none here. A bar with a label and a number beside it is a composition — a molecule — and belongs on its own page, not this one. What this page fixes is the part that is shared: round to whole percent, and where a count says more than a percentage (31 of 48 tickets), use the count. People act on counts.

When a bar earns its place

Most waits do not need one. Showing a progress bar for work that finishes before the user has focused on it is worse than showing nothing, because the flash reads as an error.

How longShowWhy
Under 1sNothing at allA bar that appears and vanishes is a flash of noise. The user was not yet waiting.
1–4sA spinner, if anythingLong enough to need acknowledging, too short for a number to be worth reading.
4–10sThis bar, determinateLong enough that the user wants to know how far along it is.
Over 10sThis bar, plus words that changeA bar alone stops reassuring. “Uploading 31 of 48” beats a bar at 64% that might be stuck.
Extent unknown, any durationIndeterminateA determinate bar whose estimate is wrong is worse than an indeterminate one that never promised anything.

The 99% problem

The fastest way to lose a user’s trust in every bar you will ever show them is to let one sit at 99% for thirty seconds. They will not remember that the number was accurate; they will remember that the bar lied.

  • Base the number on work done, not on steps completed. Four steps where the last one takes 80% of the time is a bar that races to 75 and then stops.
  • If you cannot weight the steps honestly, do not show a percentage. Use a count — step 3 of 4 — which makes no promise about time.
  • Never hold a bar at 99 waiting for a response. If the last act is out of your hands, switch to indeterminate and say what you are waiting for.
  • A bar that has not moved in ten seconds needs words. Silence and stillness together read as a hang, whatever the number says.
Speeding up is fine. Slowing down is not. A bar that accelerates toward the end feels faster than a perfectly linear one covering the same time, and a bar that decelerates feels broken. Where the estimate is soft, let it be optimistic late rather than early.

One bar, or one per item

Uploading forty-eight tickets is one job to the user and forty-eight to the system. Pick the one the user is actually waiting on.

SituationShow
A batch the user submitted as one actionOne bar for the batch, with a count in the label. They pressed one button; they are waiting for one thing.
Items that arrive and finish independentlyOne bar per row, in the row. A file list where each upload starts when it is dropped.
A batch where individual failures matterOne bar for the batch, plus per-row state after it ends. The bar answers “is it done”; the rows answer “what failed”.
Never stack more than about five bars in view. Six bars filling at different rates is a pattern nobody can read. Past five, one bar and a count.

Giving up

The bar is a readout and takes no interaction, but anything it sits beside for more than a few seconds needs a way out.

  • Anything over about ten seconds gets a Cancel beside it. A progress bar with no exit is a modal without a close button.
  • Cancel says what it will leave behind. “Cancel” on a half-finished batch should say whether the first 31 stay uploaded.
  • After a failure, the action is Retry, positioned where Cancel was. Do not make the user find the original button again.
  • Retry resumes where it can and says so. Restarting a 90%-complete upload without warning is the failure the user will actually complain about.

When it finishes

  • Hold at 100% for a beat before clearing. Roughly 1s. A bar that vanishes the instant it completes leaves the user unsure it ever did.
  • Success can disappear. Failure cannot. A completed bar may clear itself; a failed one stays until the user acknowledges it or retries.
  • Do not animate back to zero. If the same bar is reused for the next job, clear it and start fresh rather than rewinding.
  • Never leave a finished bar sitting at 100% forever. It reads as a job still in progress that happens to be nearly done.

Width and placement

  • The bar has no intrinsic width; it fills its container. Cap it at about 280px where it sits in a form, and let it run full width where it sits at the top of a panel.
  • A bar stretched across a 1600px screen makes a 2% change invisible. Wider is not clearer.
  • Put it where the work is, not where there is room. A bar at the top of the page for a change made at the bottom of a form is a bar nobody sees.
  • The 4px height pinned to the top edge of a container is the one exception — there it is deliberately peripheral, and it carries no label.

Guidelines

Do

  • Give the bar a label that names the work, and a value beside it.
  • Weight the number by work done, not by steps completed.
  • Stop the bar where it failed and say why.
  • Put a Cancel beside anything running longer than about ten seconds.
  • Pair every status colour with words that say the same thing.

Do not

  • Do not show a bar for work that finishes in under a second.
  • Do not let the fill run backwards, or hold it at 99 waiting on something else.
  • Do not show a number you are guessing at — use a count, or indeterminate.
  • Do not stack more than about five bars in view.
  • Do not use the 4px height with a label row.

Accessibility

WCAG 1.4.11 Non-text Contrast, AA asks for 3:1 on the visual information needed to understand a component. A progress bar has two such boundaries, and the old design only satisfied one: the fill against the track tells you how far along it is, and the track against the page tells you how far there is to go. Without the second, 40% and 90% look the same.

Why not simply darken the track. neutral/400 is the only Trinity neutral that clears 3:1 against both the page and the fill in both modes, so a single token swap genuinely does fix the contrast. It also makes status colour impossible: against a mid-grey track a fill needs a luminance below 0.031 or above 0.678, which means near-black or pastel. Measured against that track, error reaches only 1.70:1 and success 1.50:1. The ring gets both properties instead of trading one for the other.
  • Give the element role="progressbar" with aria-valuenow, aria-valuemin and aria-valuemax.
  • Give it an aria-label saying what is progressing. “75%” on its own tells a screen reader nothing.
  • For indeterminate, remove aria-valuenow and set aria-busy="true". Do not set it to 0.
  • Put the outcome in the label, not only in the fill colour. 1.4.1 Use of Colour, A.
  • Do not put the live percentage in an assertive live region. A number changing thirty times interrupts thirty times — announce the start and the outcome, not the journey.
  • The bar is not focusable and takes no keyboard interaction. It is a readout.
