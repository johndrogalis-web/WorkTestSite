Progress bar

An 8px bar that fills left to right. Eight variants, every one of them named by the value it shows.

Solid8 variantsLight and darkFigma 51692:17330 ↗
The contrast failure is fixed, and the fix is a 1px ring rather than a darker track. A track dark enough to clear the page on its own lands at #7F796C, and at that value no recognisable status colour can clear 3:1 against it — a fill would have to be near-black or pastel. So the track stays light and a 1px ring at #7F796C carries the bar’s extent instead. That buys both boundaries and leaves the track free for status.
This is an atom, and it stays one. The bar carries no text of its own. A label and a number belong to whatever places the bar, and the spec for them is under Anatomy so that six screens do not invent six formats.

Anatomy

  • TrackFull width of its container, 8px tall, 4px radius
  • Ring1px inside the track, #7F796C in both modes. This is what defines the bar’s full extent against the page.
  • FillSame height and radius, width set by the value
  • Height tokenprogress bar/height = 8, which is spacing/micro/200
  • Radius tokenprogress bar/radius = 4, which is radius/sm
  • Label and valueNot part of the component. Where the caller adds them: Body/sm label on the left, Body/xs value on the right, 6px clear of the track, the two ends aligned to the bar. Round to whole percent.

The label changes, and the colour follows. A bar that turns red and says nothing tells a colourblind user that something happened, not what. Every status fill is paired with label text that says the same thing in words — WCAG 1.4.1 Use of Colour, A. If you only change one of the two, change the words.

States

A progress bar has no interaction states — no hover, no focus, no disabled. It has value states, and there are three.

StateWhat it showsWhat it announces
DeterminateA fill from 0 to 100, and a number.aria-valuenow with min and max.
IndeterminateA 30% sliver travelling left to right, and no number.aria-valuenow is removed, and aria-busy="true" is set. An indeterminate bar reporting 0 is a bar claiming no progress, which is a different and wrong statement.
SettledThe fill stops and stays where it stopped, at 100 on success or wherever it failed.The final aria-valuenow, plus the outcome in the label.
A failed bar does not reset and does not keep crawling. It stops at the last real number and says what went wrong. Snapping back to 0 erases the only evidence the user has of how far the job got, and a bar that keeps creeping after the work has died is the single most distrusted pattern in any progress UI.

Behaviour

Movement

  • Width only, 240ms, cubic-bezier(.22, 1, .36, 1). The system ease. Nothing else about the bar moves — no pulse, no shimmer, no stripe.
  • Never animate backwards. If a new estimate is lower than the current fill, hold where you are until the real number catches up. A bar that goes back reads as failure even when it is just a better estimate.
  • Indeterminate travels on a 1.6s loop — a 30% sliver crossing the track. Slower than a spinner on purpose, because it is furniture, not a focal point.
  • Under prefers-reduced-motion, neither moves. The determinate bar jumps to its value; the indeterminate one becomes a static filled track at 55% opacity. Something that slides across the screen forever is exactly what that setting is for.

The number is the component’s job

  • The bar owns its value text. Making every caller hand-build a percentage beside a bar is how six screens end up with six formats.
  • Round to whole percent. Decimal places on a progress bar are noise, and they make the number twitch.
  • Where a count is more useful than a percentage — 31 of 48 tickets — use the count. People act on counts.
  • Never show a number you do not have. That is what indeterminate is for.

Determinate or not

Use a determinate bar when you know the total. Use indeterminate when you do not. Do not fake the first with the second.

  • A determinate bar whose estimate is wrong is worse than an indeterminate one that never promised anything.
  • Under about a second, show nothing at all. A bar that appears and vanishes is a flash of noise.
  • Over about ten seconds, a bar alone stops being reassuring. Add what is happening in words, and change it as the work moves.
  • For a single unknown-duration wait with no extent to show, a spinner is still the right component.

Width

  • The bar has no intrinsic width; it fills its container. Cap it at about 280px where it sits in a form, and let it run full width where it sits at the top of a panel.
  • A bar stretched across a 1600px screen makes a 2% change invisible. Wider is not clearer.

Guidelines

Do

  • Give the bar a label that names the work, and a value beside it.
  • Use it when you know the total amount of work.
  • Stop the bar where it failed and say why.
  • Cap the width. Around 280px in a form.
  • Pair every status colour with words that say the same thing.

Do not

  • Do not use it for an unknown duration without switching to indeterminate.
  • Do not let the fill run backwards.
  • Do not show a number you are guessing at.
  • Do not build a striped or shimmering variant. Neither is in the system.
  • Do not use the 4px height with a label row.

Accessibility

WCAG 1.4.11 Non-text Contrast, AA asks for 3:1 on the visual information needed to understand a component. A progress bar has two such boundaries, and the old design only satisfied one: the fill against the track tells you how far along it is, and the track against the page tells you how far there is to go. Without the second, 40% and 90% look the same.

Why not simply darken the track. neutral/400 is the only Trinity neutral that clears 3:1 against both the page and the fill in both modes, so a single token swap genuinely does fix the contrast. It also makes status colour impossible: against a mid-grey track a fill needs a luminance below 0.031 or above 0.678, which means near-black or pastel. Measured against that track, error reaches only 1.70:1 and success 1.50:1. The ring gets both properties instead of trading one for the other.
  • Give the element role="progressbar" with aria-valuenow, aria-valuemin and aria-valuemax.
  • Give it an aria-label saying what is progressing. “75%” on its own tells a screen reader nothing.
  • For indeterminate, remove aria-valuenow and set aria-busy="true". Do not set it to 0.
  • Put the outcome in the label, not only in the fill colour. 1.4.1 Use of Colour, A.
  • Do not put the live percentage in an assertive live region. A number changing thirty times interrupts thirty times — announce the start and the outcome, not the journey.
  • The bar is not focusable and takes no keyboard interaction. It is a readout.
