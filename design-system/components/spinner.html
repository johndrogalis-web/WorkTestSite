Verifi Design System/Components/Spinner

Spinner

A rotating arc for work whose duration you cannot predict. Three sizes, one colour per theme, and nothing else defined.

Has gaps3 sizesLight and darkFigma 10259:29263 ↗
It is an arc, not a stroked circle. Confirmed by Verifi Design: the component is a vector arc shape, so its thickness lives in the geometry rather than in a stroke token, and there is no separate track ring behind it. Nothing here fills with progress — it is a spinner and only a spinner. The rotation timing on this page is still a placeholder, because Figma does not specify one.

Anatomy

  • FormAn arc shape with a gap. The gap is what makes the rotation visible. It is drawn as a shape, not as a stroke on a circle, so there is no stroke-width token to look for.
  • TrackNone. There is no ring behind the arc, by design.
  • Large40 × 40, token spinner/large
  • Medium24 × 24, token spinner/medium
  • Small16 × 16, token spinner/small
  • ColourOne token, spinner/fill, flipped per theme

Choosing a size

Large40A whole page or a whole panel is waiting.

States

A spinner has one state: spinning. There is no paused, no error and no success variant, so whatever replaces the spinner when the work ends is a separate decision the system does not make for you.

Behaviour

  • Use a spinner when you do not know how long the work will take. If you do know, use the Progress bar.
  • Put the spinner where the result will appear, not somewhere else on the page. It marks the thing that is loading.
  • For work that finishes in under about half a second, show nothing. A spinner that flashes on and off reads as a glitch.
  • Keep one spinner per waiting region. Four spinners on one screen is a stalled page, not a loading page.

Guidelines

Do

  • Pair the spinner with a short line of text when the wait may be long.
  • Match the size to the area that is waiting.
  • Reserve the space the result will occupy, so nothing jumps when it arrives.

Do not

  • Do not use a spinner for a known-length operation.
  • Do not stack a spinner on top of content the person can still read.
  • Do not invent a colour for it. There is one token per theme.
  • Do not let it spin forever. Decide what a timeout looks like.

Specs

  • Sizes40 / 24 / 16
  • Colour, light#171614
  • Colour, dark#FFFFFF
  • Stroke widthNot applicable — the arc is a shape, not a stroke
  • Track ringNone, by design
  • DurationNot specified in Figma

Accessibility

The spinner passes contrast comfortably in both themes — 18.08:1 in light and 16.44:1 in dark against the product surfaces. The accessibility work here is about motion and announcement, not colour.

  • Give it role="status" and an aria-label, or put visible text beside it.
  • Announce the end of loading as well as the start. A screen reader user who hears “Loading” and then silence does not know it finished.
  • Respect prefers-reduced-motion. This page slows the rotation rather than stopping it, because a frozen spinner reads as a broken page.
  • Do not rely on the spinner alone to explain a long wait. After a few seconds, words help more than motion.

Known gaps

Two earlier questions are now closed. There is no stroke-width token because the arc is a shape rather than a stroke, and there is deliberately no track ring behind it. Both confirmed by Verifi Design.
  • No rotation duration, easing or direction. This is the one that still blocks a faithful build — two teams will pick two speeds.
  • No reduced-motion alternative is specified. This page slows the rotation rather than stopping it, which is a site decision, not spec.
  • No rule for spinner versus progress bar, and no rule for how long to wait before showing either.
  • Both demonstration frames are named “Brand core palette” in the Figma layer tree rather than after this component.
