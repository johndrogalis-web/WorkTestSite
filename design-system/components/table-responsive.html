Responsive table

The same table at desktop, tablet and phone. Figma draws the desktop table; what happens below that is the house rule already used by the Hub prototypes — the table stays a table, columns drop by priority, a chevron column appears, and the dropped columns come back inside the row when it is opened.

It is never a card. Rows do not turn into stacked cards on a phone; that pattern got pushed back by users. The header stays, the row shows only its top data points, and expanding the row shows the rest.

Breakpoints here are the real ones — 900 and 600 — measured on the table wrapper with @container, not the viewport, so a table inside a drawer or a split view breaks at the right moment too.

What changes at each width

WidthColumnsChevronWhat happens
≥ 900 desktopAllOnly if the row has Accordion Content Every column. Header sticky when the table is taller than the viewport. If columns still overflow, the wrapper scrolls sideways; cell text never wraps.
600–899 tabletMostShown Columns marked droppable leave, lowest priority first (here: Load progress). Header and rows keep their desktop size. The whole row becomes a tap target that opens it.
< 600 phoneThree or fourShown Down to the identifier, the status and the one or two columns the person scans for (here: Truck, Phase, Alerts). Header stays, compact: 12.5px text, 5px cell padding. Long values truncate.

What opens

  • The detail shows the hidden columns, and only those. Label and value, in column order, in the Accordion Content container. Visible columns do not repeat.
  • Actions that lost their column move in too. A delete or an edit that was a cell on desktop becomes a button at the foot of the detail.
  • The whole row toggles. On a tablet or a phone the chevron is the cue, not the only target; tapping anywhere on the row that is not a control opens it.
  • One open at a time on a small screen. Opening a row closes the other, so the list never becomes a wall of detail. On desktop, rows with Accordion Content may stay open together.
  • Give every droppable column a priority. Nothing is dropped that was not marked, and the identifier, the status and the alerts never are.
  • Prefer dropping a column to scrolling sideways. Sideways scroll hides the right-hand columns completely; a dropped column is one tap away.
  • Keep the <table> markup at every width. Same DOM with columns hidden, so screen readers keep the row and column semantics. data-k on every body cell is what the small layout reads as the label.
