Verifi Design System/Components/Tooltip

Tooltip

A short label that appears next to whatever you are pointing at. Four arrow positions, a box that hugs its text in both directions, and no width limit.

Has gaps4 positionsOne heightFigma 2620:4152 ↗
Figma defines the box; this page defines the behaviour. The file gives fill, text, padding, radius and four arrow positions, and stops there. A tooltip is almost entirely behaviour, so Verifi Design has asked that the Behaviour and Accessibility sections below follow established UX practice rather than sit empty. Those rules are the house convention and are binding for new work; they are marked where they go beyond what Figma says.

One thing to keep an eye on. The text node carries its own 280px cap as well as the container. It is harmless today, because the container’s 8px padding clamps the text to 264px first, but it means two numbers have to stay in step if the padding ever changes.

States

There is one state: visible. Figma does not describe how the tooltip gets there or how it leaves, so appearance, disappearance and everything in between is currently up to whoever builds it.

Behaviour

These are rules, not suggestions. Figma does not specify tooltip behaviour, so Verifi Design has adopted established UX practice as the house convention. Build to this.
  • A tooltip supplements, never carries. If the person cannot finish the task without reading it, the text belongs on the page.
  • Open after 500ms on hover, immediately on keyboard focus. The delay stops tooltips firing as the pointer crosses a toolbar; focus has no such problem, so it gets no delay.
  • Close after 100ms, and stay open while the pointer is inside the tooltip itself. That short grace period is what lets someone move onto the tooltip to read it.
  • Escape dismisses it, and it never takes focus. Focus stays on the trigger throughout.
  • One trigger open at a time. Moving between two triggers swaps immediately rather than re-running the 500ms delay.

The three cases at the real cap: a short label, a typical 44-character string that still fits on one line at 258px, and a long one that wraps to 280 × 55. This is what the component does now.

Why 280px

Not a round number picked for the look of it. At 12px in ABC Repro an average character measures about 5.8px, so 280 minus the 16px of padding holds roughly 45 characters a line.

Max widthCharacters a lineVerdict
240px38Wraps text that should have stayed on one line
280px45Recommended. The bottom of the 45–75 readable range, which is where a tooltip belongs
320px52Workable, but invites paragraphs
360px59Too much. At this width people write prose into a tooltip
  • A typical help string — “Water added automatically when slump drifts.” — measures 242px, so the common case never wraps and the cap only catches outliers.
  • 280 = 35 × 8, so it lands on the spacing scale rather than beside it.
  • Two lines at 12/1.3 plus padding comes to about 47px tall. If you need a third line, the text does not belong in a tooltip.
  • On a phone, clamp it: max-width: min(280px, calc(100vw - 32px)) so it never touches the screen edges at 375px.

Touch, and the missing width cap

Two things the convention cannot fix on its own.

  • Hover does not exist on a phone. Do not ship a pattern where a tooltip is the only route to the information. Either the label is visible at small widths, or the trigger becomes a tap target that opens a popover.
  • Set the maximum width to 280px and let it wrap. The box hugs its content on both axes, so without a cap a long tooltip becomes one enormous line. The Hug height already lets it grow downward, so the cap is the only thing missing.

Guidelines

Do

  • Keep it short. Nothing in the file stops a long tooltip becoming one very wide line.
  • Attach it to something that is already focusable.
  • Use it for a name or a short clarification.

Do not

  • Do not put essential instructions in a tooltip.
  • Do not put a link or a button inside one.
  • Do not use it as a substitute for a visible label on an icon-only control.
  • Do not rely on it existing on touch devices.

Specs

  • Max width280px, giving 264px of text and about 45 characters a line
  • HeightHug — 24px at one line
  • Padding8px
  • Arrow15 × 6 at -4px
  • Radius4px
  • Fill, light / dark#171614 / #FFFFFF
  • Text, light / dark#FFFFFF / #211F1C
  • Contrast, light / dark18.08:1 / 16.44:1
  • Show delay500ms on hover, none on focus
  • Hide delay100ms

Accessibility

Both themes clear the requirement with room to spare: white on #171614 measures 18.08:1 in light, and #211F1C on white measures 16.44:1 in dark.

  • Use aria-describedby on the trigger pointing at the tooltip, not aria-label, which would replace the trigger’s own name.
  • The tooltip must be reachable by keyboard. Hover-only is a failure of WCAG 2.1.1.
  • Content that appears on hover must be dismissible, hoverable and persistent — WCAG 1.4.13. None of the three is specified here.
  • Never put the only copy of important information inside a tooltip.

Known gaps

Three questions closed. Dark mode is specified after all — the tooltip inverts to a white fill with #211F1C text. Behaviour is settled by adopting UX best practice as the house convention, written up under Behaviour above. And the width is now capped at 280px with word-break: break-word, so long text wraps and the box grows downward instead of running off the screen.
  • The text node duplicates the container cap at 280px. Harmless while the padding is 8px, but it is a second number to maintain.
  • The arrow is drawn but not tokenised. It measures 15 × 6 at -4px, and those numbers live in the shape rather than in variables.
  • No transition is specified. The delays above are timing, not animation.
  • No stated relationship to the help text already used under form fields, which solves a neighbouring problem.
